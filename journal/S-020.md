## What went right

- **The agent served every judge itself.** With the cards visible in `claude-box`, the agent downloaded the four slow-tier GGUFs and the two new generators into `~/models`, and served each judge with one launcher. The user operated no swaps.
- **The rule and the standard slice were fixed before any run.** The selection rule was restated, and the 160-pair standard slice (LLMBar adversarial, JudgeBench code and reasoning) was cut and committed, before the first candidate's verdicts.
- **Every result was committed as it landed**, each with its Notes bullet, so the session could have died at any point without losing evidence.
- **The GPU watchdog was built and tested before the long chain ran unattended.** It never tripped. GPU1 peaked at 87 °C under Mistral-Medium.
- **Server timings are now in the verdict files.** That gives prefill and decode rates per judge. A cost question (row vs tensor split) was answered in one 24-call run: tensor split cuts Devstral's call time by 28 % with the same verdicts on 11 of 12 pairs.
- **The self-judge's standard-slice row was run, not assumed.** It turned out to be the session's main finding.

## What went wrong

- **The padded pairs were misread as part of Q-1.** Two Notes bullets and two commit messages said GLM and Devstral "fail Q-1". PLAN's Q-1 and the design (§ 4) put the padded pairs beside Q-1. `quality.py report` files them under its `q1` key, which misled. Corrected in Q-04 Notes 2026-10-23.
- **Row split was scheduled without a test.** It does not load in this llama.cpp build ("does not support split buffers"). The launcher now falls back to layer split.
- **A `pgrep -f`/kill loop matched its own shell** and killed the first chain. No harm done; the pattern is noted in this entry.
- **The download plan changed twice**: cut to GLM only, then restored at the user's word. Both are recorded in Q-04 Notes.
