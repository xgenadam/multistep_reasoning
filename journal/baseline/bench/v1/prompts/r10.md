We're migrating the order-management database at Fernhill Outfitters from MySQL 5.7 to PostgreSQL 16: about 1.8 TB, with three weeks of dual-running through change-data-capture (CDC) replication before a cutover in a four-hour Sunday maintenance window. Yesterday's risk workshop produced the list below, with likelihood (L) and impact (I) each scored from 1 (low) to 5 (high). Turn it into the risk register for the migration plan.

Workshop notes, in no particular order:
- Application queries rely on MySQL-specific behaviour (loose GROUP BY, backtick quoting, case-insensitive string comparison). L5, I3.
- The licence and support contract for the CDC tool isn't signed before dual-running starts. L4, I1.
- Rolling back to MySQL after cutover has never been rehearsed. L2, I4.
- Data-type mismatches corrupt values during the copy (zero dates, unsigned integers, ENUM columns). L4, I5.
- Reporting queries run slower on PostgreSQL until they are tuned. L2, I5.
- The cutover overruns the four-hour maintenance window. L4, I4.
- The operations team is unfamiliar with day-to-day PostgreSQL administration. L3, I2.
- CDC replication lag during dual-running lets the two databases drift apart. L3, I4.

Requirements:
- Three sections: Scoring, Risk register, Top three risks.
- Under Scoring, a short paragraph explaining the scales and that score = likelihood × impact.
- Under Risk register, one table with all eight risks and exactly these columns in this order: #, Risk, Likelihood, Impact, Score, Owner, Mitigation. Sort the rows by score, highest first, and number them 1 to 8 in that order in the # column.
- Each risk's owner is one of these roles: DBA lead, backend lead, QA lead, SRE lead, product owner.
- Each mitigation should be a concrete action, not "monitor closely".
- Under Top three risks, a numbered list of the three highest-scoring risks, each with two or three sentences on why it matters and the first thing to do about it.

The audience is the engineering managers who will sign off the plan. Aim for about 600-800 words.
