Write a Python module `justify.py` with a function `justify(text: str, width: int) -> list[str]` that fully justifies text to a fixed width.

- Words are the maximal runs of non-whitespace characters in `text` (what `text.split()` returns); line breaks and other whitespace in the input carry no meaning.
- Lines are filled greedily: each line takes as many of the remaining words as fit within `width` when separated by single spaces.
- A word longer than `width` is never split: it goes on a line of its own, unpadded, which is longer than `width`. The words before it end the previous line, and the words after it start a new line.
- Every line except the last is padded to exactly `width` characters by adding spaces between its words. The extra spaces are spread over the gaps as evenly as possible, and when they do not divide evenly the leftmost gaps get one more space than the rest. With width 16, `This is an` becomes `This    is    an` and `example of text` becomes `example  of text`.
- A line other than the last that holds a single word is that word followed by spaces up to `width`.
- The last line is left-aligned: its words are separated by single spaces, with no padding and no trailing spaces.
- The result is the list of lines, without newline characters. Text with no words gives `[]`.
- `width` less than 1 raises `ValueError`.

Use only the Python 3.12 standard library. Give each file in its own fenced code block, with the file's path on the line just before the block.
