I run a free financial-education programme at a community centre, and I want a primer that people in their twenties and thirties can read at home between our sessions. Please write it, about 7,000 words.

The readers are adults with a regular income and no financial training; some are anxious about money. Be warm, plain and non-judgemental, explain every term, and show the arithmetic so that readers can redo it with their own numbers. This is general education, not advice: do not name any bank, fund, app, insurer, card network, credit bureau or other financial company or product, and do not tell readers what they personally should buy.

**Start with a short introduction** (headed "Introduction") that explains how to use the primer and states plainly that it is general education and not financial advice.

**Then exactly ten chapters, in this order, with headings of the form "Chapter N: Title":**
1. Budgeting
2. Building an Emergency Fund
3. Managing Debt
4. Understanding Credit Scores
5. Saving and Compound Interest
6. Investing Basics
7. Inflation
8. Housing and Mortgages
9. Insurance
10. Planning for Retirement

**Worked examples.** Every chapter contains exactly one worked example, in a subsection whose heading starts "Worked Example", following one person, Maya, who is 29 and takes home €3,200 a month. Show each step of the calculation. End each worked example with a line that starts "Result:" and gives only the one value named below. Round money to the cent at the end of the calculation, not at intermediate steps, and write amounts with comma thousands separators (for example €8,144.47).

1. Budgeting: apply the 50/30/20 rule (needs / wants / savings) to Maya's take-home pay. Result: the monthly savings amount.
2. Emergency fund: Maya's essential costs are €1,850 a month and she wants six months of essential costs saved. She saves her 50/30/20 savings amount every month. Result: the number of whole months it takes her to reach the target (round up).
3. Debt: Maya has a credit-card balance of €4,000 at 21% APR, with interest charged monthly at one-twelfth of the APR. She pays €200 this month. Result: how much of that €200 payment reduces the balance (the payment minus the month's interest).
4. Credit scores: Maya's cards have a combined limit of €5,000 and a combined balance of €1,500. Result: her credit utilisation, as a percentage.
5. Compound interest: Maya puts €5,000 into savings at 5% interest a year, compounded annually, for 10 years. Result: the balance after 10 years.
6. Investing: €100,000 invested for 20 years at a 6% annual return before fees, compounded annually. Compare annual fees of 0.2% and 1.0% (subtract the fee from the return: 5.8% and 5.0%). Result: how much more the low-fee investment is worth after 20 years.
7. Inflation: Maya's yearly living costs are €40,000 in today's money. Assume 2.5% inflation a year for 20 years. Result: what the same living costs will be in 20 years.
8. Mortgages: a €250,000 repayment mortgage at 4% a year over 25 years, with monthly payments, using the formula payment = P × r / (1 − (1 + r)^−n), where r is the monthly rate (4% ÷ 12) and n is the number of months. Result: the monthly payment.
9. Insurance: Policy A costs €480 a year with a €250 excess; Policy B costs €360 a year with a €1,000 excess. Result: the number of years per claim at which the two policies cost the same (that is, Policy B's yearly saving divided into its extra excess).
10. Retirement: Maya saves €2,400 at the end of every year for 30 years at 6% a year, compounded annually, using the future-value formula FV = payment × ((1 + i)^n − 1) / i. Result: the value after 30 years.

**Each chapter** also explains the key ideas behind its example, the common mistakes people make, and ends with a subsection headed "Key Takeaways" containing three to five bullets.

**End with a section titled "Glossary"** defining at least 20 terms used in the primer, as a bulleted list with one term per bullet.
