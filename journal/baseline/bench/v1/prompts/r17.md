Draft an acceptable-use policy for company laptops at Brannock Design, a 60-person design studio. Every employee gets a company-owned laptop that IT manages remotely, with full-disk encryption switched on. Staff will be asked to read and sign it, so it has to be short, clear and fair, and written for designers, not lawyers.

Facts the policy must reflect:
- Staff may use their laptop for reasonable personal use (email, browsing, personal documents) as long as it doesn't interfere with work or break any other clause.
- Only software from the company's self-service app catalogue may be installed; anything else needs IT's approval first.
- Client files are stored only in the company's cloud drive, never on USB sticks or personal cloud accounts.
- The laptop must lock after 5 minutes of inactivity, and must never be left unattended in a public place.
- A lost or stolen laptop must be reported to IT (it@brannock.example) within 24 hours, so it can be wiped remotely.
- Laptops are returned to IT within 5 working days of an employee's last day.
- Breaking the policy may lead to the laptop being restricted or recalled and, for serious or repeated breaches, to the disciplinary procedure.

Requirements:
- Use exactly these three headings: "Using your laptop", "Keeping client work safe", "Loss, leaving and enforcement".
- Write the policy as numbered clauses, 1 to 12, numbered continuously across the three headings (do not restart the numbering under each heading).
- Include a clause on enforcement.
- Plain language: say "must" rather than "shall", and avoid legal terms such as "hereby", "herein", "pursuant to" and "thereof".
- Aim for about 700-900 words, including a short introduction saying who the policy applies to.
