I run people operations at Harbourlight Analytics, a 30-person data consultancy with one office in a mid-sized city and a handful of people who work mostly from home. We have grown from eight people to thirty in eighteen months and our policies currently live in a dozen scattered documents and a lot of people's heads. I need a proper employee handbook that a new hire can read in their first week and that a manager can point to when a question comes up.

Please write the complete handbook, about 7,000 words in total. Write it in plain, warm, direct English, addressed to the employee as "you". It should sound like a small company that trusts its people, not like a legal contract, but it must still be precise wherever a rule or a number is involved.

Use exactly these ten chapter titles, in this order, as the chapter headings:

1. Welcome and Our Values
2. How We Work
3. Employment Basics
4. Pay and Benefits
5. Leave and Time Off
6. Health, Safety and Wellbeing
7. Equipment, Security and Data
8. Code of Conduct
9. Growth, Feedback and Reviews
10. Leaving Harbourlight

Here are the facts the handbook must reflect. Use these figures exactly as given, written in digits, and do not invent any other entitlements, amounts or deadlines.

**Values (chapter 1).** Our four values are: Be candid, Own the outcome, Make it simple, Leave room for others. Explain what each one means in practice, with an everyday example of each.

**How we work (chapter 2).** Core hours are 10:00–16:00, Monday to Friday; outside core hours people arrange their own day. The standard working week is 37.5 hours. Team stand-ups are at 10:15. We use one chat tool and one shared calendar; urgent requests go by phone, not chat. People in the office or at home are treated the same.

**Employment basics (chapter 3).** Every new starter has a 3-month probation period with a check-in at the end of each month. Notice during probation is 1 week on either side; after probation it is 4 weeks on either side. Contracts are permanent unless a fixed term is stated in the offer letter. Personal details changes go to People Operations within 2 weeks.

**Pay and benefits (chapter 4).** Salaries are paid monthly on the 25th (or the last working day before it). Salary bands are reviewed every April. Benefits: a personal learning budget of €1,200 per year; a one-off home-office allowance of €500 for new starters; private health insurance from day one; employer pension contribution of 6% of salary, matched against at least 3% from the employee; a cycle-to-work scheme.

**Leave and time off (chapter 5).** There are seven types of leave:
- Annual leave: 25 days per year, plus 8 public holidays. Up to 5 unused days can be carried over, and carried-over days must be used by 31 March.
- Sick leave: 10 days per year on full pay; a doctor's note is needed after 5 consecutive days of absence.
- Parental leave (primary carer): 16 weeks on full pay.
- Partner leave: 4 weeks on full pay, to be taken within the first 6 months.
- Bereavement leave: 3 days, or 5 days for a partner, child or parent.
- Jury and public duty leave: paid at full salary for as long as the duty lasts.
- Unpaid leave: up to 20 days per year, by agreement with your manager.

Requests for annual leave longer than 3 consecutive days need at least 2 weeks' notice. The Leave and Time Off chapter must contain a summary table (the only table in that chapter) with exactly seven rows, one per leave type, with columns for the leave type, the entitlement and how to request it.

**Health, safety and wellbeing (chapter 6).** A trained first-aider is named on the office noticeboard. Everyone can use the confidential employee assistance line, free, at any time. Home workers do a short workstation self-assessment in their first month.

**Equipment, security and data (chapter 7).** Everyone gets a company laptop, which must have full-disk encryption and automatic screen lock after 5 minutes. Client data stays on company systems and is never copied to personal devices. Lost or stolen equipment is reported to IT within 1 hour. Password manager use is mandatory.

**Code of conduct (chapter 8).** Open the chapter with exactly eight numbered conduct commitments, written by you from our values and the norms of a professional consultancy. Use bullet points, not numbers, for any other list in that chapter. Explain how to raise a concern, including anonymously, and say that nobody will be penalised for raising a concern in good faith.

**Growth, feedback and reviews (chapter 9).** Reviews happen twice a year, in April and October. Everyone has a monthly one-to-one with their manager. Promotions are discussed at the April review.

**Leaving Harbourlight (chapter 10).** Return equipment on your last day; you get an exit conversation with People Operations; unused annual leave is paid out; references confirm dates and role.

Other requirements:
- End every chapter with a short line that begins "Who to ask:" and names the role (not a person) to contact about that chapter's topics, for example People Operations, your manager, IT or Finance.
- Never describe the company as a family.
- Keep it practical: where there is a process, tell the reader what to do, step by step.
