My partner and I are opening Ember & Oak Coffee Roasters, a small-batch speciality roastery in Halbrook, a city of about 400,000 people. We will present a business plan to our bank next month to support a loan application, and we will also use it to run the business for the first three years. Please write the plan, about 7,000 words in total, for a bank lending officer who knows small businesses but not coffee. It should be confident but realistic, and every number in it must come from the figures below or be calculated from them.

Use exactly these ten sections, in this order, as the section headings:

1. Executive Summary
2. Company and Products
3. Market Analysis
4. Competition
5. Operations
6. Marketing and Sales
7. Management and Staffing
8. Financial Plan
9. Risks and Mitigations
10. Funding Request

**About us.** Two founders: Priya Anand (eight years as head roaster at a regional roastery) and Tom Keller (ten years in hospitality sales and café management). We will lease a 180 m² light-industrial unit and roast on one 15 kg drum roaster. We sell three ways: wholesale to independent cafés and restaurants, an online subscription (one 1 kg delivery per month), and retail bags at the city's two weekly farmers' markets. We buy green coffee from two importers, with traceable single-origin lots.

**Market facts.** Halbrook has 180 independent cafés and 65 chain outlets. A survey of 40 independent café owners we ran found that 22 were open to switching roaster if offered fresher coffee and barista training. The nearest speciality roastery is 70 km away; two national roasters supply most cafés in the city. Our target is 30 wholesale accounts by the end of Year 3.

**Revenue inputs.**
- Wholesale: 6,000 kg in Year 1, 9,000 kg in Year 2 and 12,000 kg in Year 3, at €22 per kg in every year.
- Online subscriptions: an average of 300 subscribers in Year 1, 550 in Year 2 and 800 in Year 3, each paying €18 per month for 12 months.
- Farmers' markets: €30,000 in Year 1, €36,000 in Year 2 and €40,000 in Year 3.

**Cost inputs.**
- Cost of goods sold (green coffee, packaging, shipping of subscriptions): 38% of total revenue in every year.
- Rent: €24,000 per year.
- Salaries (including the founders): €72,000 in Year 1, €98,000 in Year 2, €124,000 in Year 3.
- Marketing: €15,000 in Year 1, €18,000 in Year 2, €20,000 in Year 3.
- Utilities and insurance: €9,000 per year.
- Depreciation: the roaster costs €60,000 and is depreciated straight-line over 5 years.
- Ignore tax, interest and inflation in the tables.

**Start-up costs and funding.** Roaster €60,000; unit fit-out and ventilation €25,000; working capital €20,000. The founders invest €45,000 of their own money and ask the bank for the rest as a five-year loan.

**Financial Plan requirements.** The Financial Plan section must contain these two tables, with the columns Item, Year 1, Year 2, Year 3, Three-year total, in that order. Write amounts as whole euros with comma thousands separators (for example 132,000), and make every total add up exactly.
- A revenue table with exactly these four rows: Wholesale, Subscriptions, Farmers' markets, Total revenue.
- A profit and loss table with exactly these rows: Total revenue, Cost of goods sold, Gross profit, Rent, Salaries, Marketing, Utilities and insurance, Depreciation, Total operating expenses, Operating profit.

Also state the gross margin as a percentage and the operating margin for each year, rounded to one decimal place, and explain in words what drives the change in operating profit from Year 1 to Year 3.

**Other requirements.**
- Risks and Mitigations: a table with exactly six risks, each with likelihood, impact and mitigation.
- Funding Request: state the total start-up cost, the founders' contribution and the loan amount requested, and explain from the profit figures why the business can service the loan.
- Do not invent customer names, quotes or statistics beyond those given.
