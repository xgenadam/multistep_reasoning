Our security team keeps asking department managers to approve things like "rotating the signing keys" and "moving the private keys into a hardware module", and the managers have no mental model for what any of it means. Write a short explainer of public-key cryptography for them: non-technical managers who are smart, busy and want to know what matters for their decisions.

Requirements:
- About 600-800 words.
- Build the explanation around one analogy, and stick with that single analogy throughout rather than switching between several.
- No equations or mathematical notation of any kind.
- Don't name specific algorithms or standards; the managers don't need them.
- Include a section on digital signatures (what signing proves, and what it doesn't) and a section on what happens if a private key is lost or stolen.
- End with a section titled "Questions to ask your team" containing exactly three numbered questions a manager could ask before approving a key-related change.

Use clear section headings. The tone should be plain and confident, not condescending.
