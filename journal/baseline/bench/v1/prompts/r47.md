I run security operations at Northgate Outfitters, an online outdoor-clothing retailer with about 250 staff, a web shop, two warehouses and a small in-house IT and security team (six people). We need a set of incident response playbooks that an on-call engineer can open at 3 a.m. and follow without reading anything else. Please write them, about 7,500 words in total.

Because each playbook will be opened on its own during an incident, every playbook must be complete in itself. Steps that apply to several scenarios (opening an incident log, appointing an incident commander, preserving evidence, briefing leadership) must be written out in full in each playbook where they apply, not cross-referenced. Write in short imperative steps; say who does each step by role; and say what "done" looks like for each phase.

**Start with one short section titled "How to Use These Playbooks"** (no more than about 500 words), containing:
- a severity table with exactly three rows:
  - SEV1: customer-facing outage, active attacker, or confirmed exposure of customer data; all-hands response, leadership informed within 30 minutes.
  - SEV2: contained compromise of one account or system, or a suspected data exposure; response in working hours plus on-call.
  - SEV3: low-impact event with no evidence of compromise; next working day.
- the roles, as a bulleted list: Incident Commander (the on-call security engineer, until handed over), Communications Lead (Head of Customer Service), Technical Lead (on-call platform engineer), Data Protection Officer, Executive Sponsor (the COO).

**Then exactly eight playbooks, in this order, each with its title as the heading:**
1. Ransomware on a File Server (default severity SEV1)
2. Compromised Employee Email Account (SEV2)
3. Lost or Stolen Laptop (SEV3)
4. DDoS Attack on the Web Shop (SEV1)
5. Leaked Cloud Access Key (SEV1)
6. Insider Data Theft (SEV2)
7. Customer Data Exposed through the Web Shop (SEV1)
8. Breach at a Third-Party Supplier (SEV2)

**Each playbook** begins with a line in the form "Default severity: SEVn", followed by a line naming the typical triggers. Then it has exactly five subsections, as headings, in this order: Detection, Containment, Eradication, Recovery, Communications. Use numbered steps in each subsection.

**Facts to use.**
- The incident log is a new ticket in the "SEC-INC" queue, created at the start of every incident.
- Evidence: take memory and disk images before rebuilding any machine; never power off a machine suspected of ransomware infection, isolate it from the network instead.
- Laptops have full-disk encryption and can be remotely locked and wiped from the device-management console.
- Cloud access keys can be revoked by the platform team in under 5 minutes; all key use is logged.
- The web shop is behind a content-delivery network with rate limiting, and the provider's emergency DDoS line is in the on-call runbook.
- Where personal data may have been exposed, the Data Protection Officer decides whether to notify the regulator, which must happen within 72 hours of becoming aware of the breach, and whether to notify affected customers.
- Backups: nightly, kept for 35 days, with an offline copy weekly; restores are tested quarterly.
- Customer communications only go out after approval by the Communications Lead and the Executive Sponsor.

**End with a section titled "After Every Incident"** that describes the post-incident review: held within 5 working days, blameless, with actions tracked in the SEC-INC ticket.
