I'm applying for a small agricultural research grant and need the project abstract. Here are my notes on the project:

- Problem: smallholder farmers in semi-arid regions mostly irrigate on a fixed schedule or by eye, which wastes water in cool weeks and stresses crops in hot ones. Commercial soil-moisture monitoring systems cost far more than a smallholding can justify.
- Idea: a soil-moisture sensor we have built from off-the-shelf parts for about $14 per unit, read by a basic mobile phone over SMS, which texts the farmer a simple "irrigate today / wait" advisory based on the reading and a three-day weather forecast.
- Study design: 120 farms growing maize in one district, randomly assigned, half using the advisories and half continuing their usual practice, over two growing seasons.
- Measures: water used per hectare (from metered pumps), maize yield per hectare, and whether farmers still follow the advisories in the second season.
- Expected result: based on our pilot on 8 farms, roughly 20 % less water with no loss of yield.
- Why it matters: if it works, the sensor is cheap enough for farmer cooperatives to buy without subsidies.

Requirements:
- Use the markdown heading `## Abstract` for the abstract and `## Keywords` for the keywords.
- The abstract must be between 230 and 250 words: the grant portal rejects anything outside that range.
- Include the sensor cost, the number of farms and the two growing seasons.
- Exactly five keywords, as a bulleted list.
- No citations or references; the application has a separate literature section.

Write it for a review panel of agricultural scientists and development economists: precise, plain and free of hype, and in the future tense where it describes work not yet done.
