I am the information security manager at Fenwick & Shaw Logistics, a freight-forwarding company with about 400 staff across three depots and a head office. Our auditors have asked us to replace a single out-of-date "IT rules" document with a proper suite of security policies. Each policy will be published as its own page on the intranet and signed off by a different owner, so each one has to make sense when read on its own, without the others.

Please write the six policies below, about 6,500 words in total. Write for ordinary staff, not security specialists: clear, firm and specific. Use "must" for mandatory requirements and "should" only for recommendations.

The six policies, in this order, with these titles as their headings:

1. Acceptable Use Policy (owner: Head of IT)
2. Access Control Policy (owner: Information Security Manager)
3. Password and Authentication Policy (owner: Information Security Manager)
4. Data Classification and Handling Policy (owner: Data Protection Officer)
5. Remote Access Policy (owner: Head of IT)
6. Security Incident Reporting Policy (owner: Information Security Manager)

**Structure of every policy.** Each policy begins with a line "Owner: <role>" and a line "Review: annually", then has exactly these five subsections, as headings, in this order: Definitions, Purpose, Scope, Policy, Enforcement.

**Shared definitions.** Because each policy is read on its own, the Definitions subsection of every policy repeats the four shared terms below, in the same wording each time, followed by any terms specific to that policy. Present definitions as a bulleted list, not a table.
- Company information: any information created, received or held by Fenwick & Shaw in any form.
- User: any employee, contractor, temporary worker or third party given access to company systems.
- Company system: any device, application, network or service owned, leased or managed by Fenwick & Shaw.
- System owner: the named manager accountable for a company system's access and security.

**Facts each policy must reflect** (use these figures exactly):
- Acceptable use: limited personal use of company systems is allowed outside working time; company email must not be auto-forwarded to personal accounts; unapproved software must not be installed; monitoring of company systems happens and users must be told so.
- Access control: access follows least privilege and is approved by the system owner; access rights are reviewed quarterly; leavers' accounts are disabled within 24 hours of their last day; shared accounts are not allowed except for named exceptions recorded by the Information Security Manager; administrator rights are held on separate accounts.
- Passwords and authentication: passwords are at least 14 characters; multi-factor authentication (MFA) is required for email, remote access and every administrator account; accounts lock after 10 failed sign-in attempts; passwords are changed only when compromise is suspected, not on a schedule; a company-provided password manager must be used.
- Data classification: exactly four levels, from lowest to highest: Public, Internal, Confidential, Restricted. Customer shipment manifests are Confidential; staff medical and payroll data are Restricted. Restricted information must be encrypted in storage and in transit and must never be sent by ordinary email. The Data Classification and Handling Policy contains a table with exactly four rows, one per level, giving examples and handling rules; it is the only table in that policy.
- Remote access: remote access only through the company VPN with MFA; personal devices may reach webmail only, never the VPN; public Wi-Fi is allowed only with the VPN on; screens must be locked when unattended.
- Incident reporting: every suspected security incident must be reported to the IT Service Desk (extension 4400) within 1 hour of discovery; staff must not investigate on their own or delete suspicious emails before reporting; personal data breaches are assessed by the Data Protection Officer, who decides on notifying the regulator within 72 hours; nobody is disciplined for reporting a genuine mistake promptly.

**Enforcement.** Every Enforcement subsection states that breaches are handled under the disciplinary procedure HR-07, and says how exceptions are requested (in writing to the policy owner, recorded, time-limited to a maximum of 12 months).

Do not add a preamble or an introduction to the suite; start directly with the first policy. Do not name any software vendor or product.
