Our product team (product managers and designers with no distributed-systems background) keeps hearing "CAP theorem" in design reviews and wants to understand it well enough to make product calls. Write an explainer for them. We run every service in two regions, the EU and the US.

Use these markdown sections: The trade-off, Scenario 1: Flash-sale shopping cart, Scenario 2: Concert seat booking, Scenario 3: Likes counter, Glossary.

- The trade-off: what CAP says in plain language (when the network between the regions breaks, a system must either refuse or delay some requests to stay consistent, or keep answering and risk regions disagreeing), and the common misreading that you simply "pick any two".
- The three scenarios, with what the business has told us:
  1. Flash-sale shopping cart: losing an item a customer added to their cart is far worse than the cart briefly showing a stale item; stock is checked again at checkout.
  2. Concert seat booking: two fans must never be sold the same seat; a "please try again" message is acceptable.
  3. Likes counter: the count on a post may lag by a few seconds or differ briefly between regions, but the like button must always respond.
- In each scenario, explain what users would see during a network split under each choice, and end the section with a line reading either "Our choice: consistency" or "Our choice: availability".
- Glossary: exactly five bullet points, defining partition, consistency, availability, eventual consistency and latency, each in one or two sentences.

No code and no maths; use everyday analogies where they help. Keep it to 800-1,000 words.
