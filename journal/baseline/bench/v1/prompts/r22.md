Our team is about to build the backend for Pawprint, appointment and records software for small veterinary clinics, and we're split between PostgreSQL and a document database (MongoDB-style) as the primary data store. The facts:

- Core data: clinics, staff, pet owners, pets, appointments, invoices and payments. Invoices must always agree with payments; clinic accountants reconcile them every month.
- Medical records vary by species and by clinic: a dog's vaccination record, a horse's dental chart and a reptile's habitat note share almost no fields, and clinics add their own custom fields.
- Clinics want reports such as revenue per vet per month, no-show rate by weekday, and overdue vaccinations across all their pets.
- Expected scale in year 3: 400 clinics, about 30 million appointments and 2 TB of data in total, with a peak of 300 writes per second.
- The team: five backend engineers; four have run PostgreSQL in production, one has used MongoDB.
- Hosting: a managed cloud database either way, and the budget allows for one database, not two.

Write a recommendation for our engineering manager, who will make the call and needs to defend it to the CTO. Use these markdown sections: Context, Comparison, Recommendation, What would change this recommendation.

- Comparison: a table with one row for each of these six criteria: transactional consistency, schema flexibility, reporting and ad-hoc queries, team experience, operational cost, scaling to the expected load. Give a column for each option and a column saying which option the row favours.
- Recommendation: choose one primary store, and explain how it copes with the criteria where it is weaker.
- What would change this recommendation: exactly two conditions, as a numbered list, each specific enough that the manager could tell whether it has become true.

Be even-handed in the comparison and decisive in the recommendation. Keep it to 700-900 words.
