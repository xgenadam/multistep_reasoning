Write a small double-entry bookkeeping library as a Python package `books/` with four files: `books/__init__.py`, `books/accounts.py`, `books/journal.py` and `books/statements.py`. It keeps a chart of accounts as a tree, posts balanced journal entries, and prints a trial balance and an income statement.

Money is `decimal.Decimal`. Wherever an amount is accepted, a `Decimal`, an `int` or a numeric `str` is converted to `Decimal`; a `float` raises `TypeError`, and a negative amount or one with more than two decimal places raises `ValueError`. Days are `datetime.date`.

## `books/accounts.py`

- `AccountType`: an enum with members `ASSET`, `LIABILITY`, `EQUITY`, `INCOME` and `EXPENSE`, whose values are the lowercase names (`"asset"`, ...). Assets and expenses are debit-normal; the other three are credit-normal.
- `Account`: a frozen dataclass `(code: str, name: str, type: AccountType, parent: str | None)`.
- `Chart()`:
  - `add(code, name, type, parent=None) -> Account`. `code` is a non-empty string of digits; `type` is an `AccountType` or its value string. It raises `ValueError` for a bad code, an unknown type, a code that already exists, a parent that does not exist, or a parent of a different type.
  - `get(code) -> Account`, raising `KeyError` for an unknown code.
  - `children(code) -> list[Account]`, sorted by code; `roots() -> list[Account]`, the accounts without a parent, sorted by code; `is_leaf(code) -> bool`.
  - `walk() -> list[tuple[Account, int]]`: every account with its depth (0 for a root), depth first: each account comes right before its children, and roots and siblings go in code order.

## `books/journal.py`

- `UnbalancedEntryError(ValueError)`.
- `Line`: a frozen dataclass `(account: str, debit=Decimal(0), credit=Decimal(0))`.
- `Entry`: a frozen dataclass `(number: int, day: datetime.date, memo: str, lines: tuple[Line, ...])`. Its lines hold the converted `Decimal` amounts.
- `LedgerRow`: a `NamedTuple` `(number, day, memo, debit, credit, balance)`.
- `Journal(chart)`:
  - `post(day, memo, lines) -> Entry` checks, in this order, and records nothing if a check fails:
    1. there are at least two lines (`ValueError`);
    2. each line's account exists (`KeyError`) and is a leaf (`ValueError`);
    3. each line's amounts are valid (see above), and exactly one of `debit` and `credit` is non-zero (`ValueError`);
    4. the debits and credits total the same (`UnbalancedEntryError`).

    Entries are numbered 1, 2, 3, ... in posting order. They may be posted in any day order.
  - `entries() -> list[Entry]` in number order.
  - `balance(code, start=None, end=None) -> Decimal`: the account's balance on its normal side (debits − credits for a debit-normal account, credits − debits otherwise), counting its own lines and those of all its descendants, from entries with `start <= day <= end` (either bound may be `None`). It can be negative. `KeyError` for an unknown code.
  - `ledger(code) -> list[LedgerRow]`: one row per line posted to the account or a descendant, in order of day, then entry number, then line position in the entry. `balance` is the running balance on the account's normal side after that row.

## `books/statements.py`

Both statements are strings of lines, each ending in `\n`, and both print accounts the same way:
- an account line is two spaces per depth level, then `<code> <name>: <amount>`;
- the amount is the account's balance over the statement's period (its own lines plus all descendants'), with exactly two decimals and a leading `-` when negative;
- an account is shown only if it or a descendant has at least one line in the period, and accounts are shown in `walk()` order.

`trial_balance(journal, as_of=None) -> str`:
- The first line is `TRIAL BALANCE as of YYYY-MM-DD`, or just `TRIAL BALANCE` when `as_of` is `None`. The period is every entry with `day <= as_of` (all entries when `None`).
- Then the account lines for the whole chart.
- Then `Total debits: <amount>` and `Total credits: <amount>`. For every account that has lines of its own, take debits − credits over the period: the positive results sum to the total debits, and the negative ones (as positive amounts) to the total credits.

`income_statement(journal, start, end) -> str` covers entries with `start <= day <= end` (`ValueError` if `start > end`). Lines in order:
- `INCOME STATEMENT YYYY-MM-DD to YYYY-MM-DD`
- `Income`, then the account lines of the income accounts, then `Total income: <amount>`;
- `Expenses`, then the account lines of the expense accounts, then `Total expenses: <amount>`;
- `Net income: <amount>`, which is total income − total expenses and may be negative.

The totals are the sums of the shown root accounts of that type, and are `0.00` when none is shown. For example:

```
INCOME STATEMENT 2026-01-01 to 2026-03-31
Income
4000 Revenue: 1200.00
  4100 Sales: 1000.00
  4200 Services: 200.00
Total income: 1200.00
Expenses
5000 Expenses: 700.00
  5100 Rent: 600.00
  5300 Supplies: 100.00
Total expenses: 700.00
Net income: 500.00
```

`books/__init__.py` exports `AccountType`, `Account`, `Chart`, `Line`, `Entry`, `LedgerRow`, `Journal`, `UnbalancedEntryError`, `trial_balance` and `income_statement`.

Use only the Python 3.12 standard library. Give each file in its own fenced code block, with the file's path on the line just before the block.
