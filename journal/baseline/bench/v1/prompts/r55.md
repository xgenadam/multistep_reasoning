I am the IT continuity lead at Halden Mutual, a regional insurer with 600 staff. Our regulator expects an up-to-date, tested disaster recovery (DR) plan, and ours dates from before we moved half our systems to a second site. Please write the new DR plan, about 6,500 words.

The readers are the IT staff who will carry it out under pressure, the managers who own each system, and the auditors who will check it. Be precise and operational: numbered steps, named roles, clear decision points and measurable targets.

**Infrastructure facts.**
- Primary data centre in our head office building in Halden. Secondary site: a leased data centre 180 km away.
- Tier 1 systems run warm standby at the secondary site, with continuous database replication (typical lag under 1 minute) and failover by DNS and load-balancer changes.
- Tier 2 systems are restored at the secondary site from storage snapshots taken every 4 hours and replicated there.
- Tier 3 systems are restored from nightly backups. Backups are kept for 35 days, and a weekly immutable copy is kept offline for 1 year.
- All recovery runbooks are stored in the secondary site's wiki and as printed copies in the DR cabinet at both sites.

**Tier definitions.**
- Tier 1: RTO up to 4 hours, or RPO up to 15 minutes.
- Tier 2: RTO up to 24 hours, or RPO up to 4 hours.
- Tier 3: everything else.

Assign each system to the most demanding tier whose condition it meets: Tier 1 if its RTO is 4 hours or less or its RPO is 15 minutes or less; otherwise Tier 2 if its RTO is 24 hours or less or its RPO is 4 hours or less; otherwise Tier 3.
