Maeve Doyle owns Larkspur Bakery and is deciding what to focus on next year. Write her a SWOT analysis from these facts:

- Two shops in the market town of Kelbridge, opened in 2014 and 2021.
- Known for its sourdough; a 4.8-star average rating from about 1,900 online reviews.
- Nine staff. The head baker, Tom, retires in 12 months and nobody has been trained to replace him.
- A single deck oven, which is at full capacity on Saturdays; the shops turn away about 40 orders every Saturday.
- Rent on the second shop rises 18 % next April.
- Flour costs are up 22 % on last year, and Larkspur hasn't raised its prices in two years. Gross margin has fallen from 64 % to 58 %.
- A business park with about 1,200 office workers opens 400 metres from the first shop next spring.
- Two local cafés have asked about a wholesale sourdough supply, about 120 loaves a week between them.
- A supermarket with an in-store bakery opens on the high street in the summer.
- Online pre-orders are 6 % of sales; the ordering page is clunky and there is no delivery.
- The bakery's Instagram account has 4,800 followers and posts about once a week.

Use these markdown sections: SWOT matrix, Strategies, Summary.

- SWOT matrix: a 2×2 markdown table. The header row has an empty first cell, then Helpful and Harmful. A row labelled Internal holds Strengths and Weaknesses; a row labelled External holds Opportunities and Threats. Put three or four points in each cell, labelled S1, S2, … W1, … O1, … T1, …, separated by <br>. Use only the facts above.
- Strategies: exactly three, as a numbered list. Each one pairs a strength or opportunity with a weakness or threat, and starts with the labels it links in brackets, for example (S2 + T1). Make them concrete enough to act on within the year.
- Summary: two or three sentences on where Maeve should focus first.

Write for a busy owner, not a business-school audience. Aim for about 500-700 words.
