Our family's home network has grown into something only I understand, and I travel for work. I want a troubleshooting guide that my partner and our two teenagers can follow on their own when something goes wrong, printed and kept in a folder next to the router. Please write it, about 6,500 words.

The readers are sensible but not technical: explain every term the first time you use it, keep each step to one action, tell them what they should see if the step worked, and tell them when to stop and call me or the internet provider instead of going further. Never ask them to do anything that could lock everyone out (for example, a factory reset of the router) without first saying clearly that it is a last resort and what it will undo.

**Our setup.**
- Internet: a fibre line. The provider's box on the hallway wall (the "fibre box") has four lights: POWER, PON, LOS and LAN. Normal is POWER, PON and LAN green, and LOS off. A red LOS light means the fibre signal is lost.
- Router: in the hallway cupboard, connected to the fibre box by a grey cable. Its admin page is at http://192.168.50.1 and the admin password is on a card taped inside the cupboard door. It hands out addresses from 192.168.50.100 to 192.168.50.199.
- Wi-Fi: two names. "Larkspur" is the main network (2.4 GHz and 5 GHz combined); "Larkspur-Guest" is for visitors and smart-home gadgets.
- Mesh: two mesh points, one on the landing upstairs and one in the garden office, which extend Larkspur. Each has one light: solid white is good, flashing yellow means it has lost the router.
- Devices: two Windows laptops, one MacBook, four phones (two iPhone, two Android), a smart TV in the living room, a games console connected by cable, a network printer in the office, and six smart plugs on the guest network.
- The internet provider's support line is 0800 555 0199, and the account number is on the same card in the cupboard.

**Inside every problem section,** use exactly three subsections, as headings, in this order: "Symptoms" (a short bulleted list), "Diagnosis" (numbered steps), and "Fix" (numbered steps, simplest and safest first). Where a step uses a command on a computer, show the exact command in a code block and say what output to expect, for both Windows and macOS where they differ.

**Before the problems,** add a section titled "Start Here: Which Problem Do I Have?". It is a decision flow written as a numbered list of yes/no questions; every answer either points to the next question or to a problem by its number, written as "Problem N". Every one of the fifteen problems must be reachable from it.

**After the problems,** add a section titled "When to Call for Help" with exactly five bullet points, each one a situation in which they should stop and call me or the provider.
