**What the service does.** Riders request a ride from the app. The dispatch service finds nearby available drivers, ranks them, and offers the ride to one driver at a time. A driver has 10 seconds to accept; if they decline or the offer times out, the next-ranked driver gets the offer. Drivers' apps send their location while they are online. The service also keeps location history for safety investigations and for pricing analysis.

**Load figures (use these exactly).**
- Peak number of drivers online at the same time: 60,000.
- Each online driver's app sends one location update every 4 seconds.
- Average number of drivers online, averaged over a 24-hour day: 25,000.
- Each location update is 200 bytes on the wire and in storage.
- Rides per day: 1,800,000. The busiest hour carries 12% of the day's rides.
- On average a ride needs 3 dispatch offers before a driver accepts.
- Location history is kept for 30 days.
- Each completed ride produces one ride record of 2,000 bytes, kept for one year (365 days).
- One location-ingest node can handle 5,000 location updates per second. Size the ingest tier for 2× the peak rate.
- Use decimal units: 1 MB = 1,000,000 bytes, 1 GB = 1,000,000,000 bytes, 1 TB = 1,000 GB.

**Requirements to carry into section 2.**
- Dispatch decision (from ride request to the first offer sent to a driver): p99 under 2 seconds.
- Availability of ride requests and dispatch: 99.95% per month.
- A driver must never hold two active offers at once, and a ride must never be accepted by two drivers.
- A location update must be visible to matching within 1 second of arrival.
- Location data is personal data: access to history needs a recorded reason, and history older than 30 days is deleted, not archived.
